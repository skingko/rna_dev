---
name: Evidence based research
description: Compare primary evidence, preserve provenance and mark uncertainty.
---

Use when a task requires explaining, comparing or making a recommendation from evidence. Start with the actual question and the decision it informs. Use available project files and assigned retrieval tools; do not claim current internet knowledge without retrieving it.

1. Identify the important claims and the evidence needed to establish or falsify each. Locate primary material and read the relevant sections, not only snippets. Record the source, date, precise location and retrieval limits. Treat an inaccessible source as missing, not as evidence for your prior belief.
2. Build a compact evidence ledger: claim, direct observation, source, population/sample, variable, unit, period, limitations and contrary evidence. Distinguish what the source reports from your calculation or inference.
3. Compare like with like. Check definitions, denominators, sampling and time windows before merging findings. A current-user sample is not necessarily a new-user sample. Transactions are not unique customers. Self-reports are not independently observed behavior. Preserve these distinctions throughout the prose.
4. Reproduce important calculations and check sensitivity to missing values or assumptions. Correlation needs a meaningful joint comparison; simultaneous observations of different variables do not by themselves establish a trend. Causal conclusions need a justified identification strategy, not merely before/after values.
5. Draft a direct answer supported by specific citations. State what is known, what is inferred, what remains unknown and what evidence would change the conclusion. Discuss material disagreement instead of averaging incompatible studies or selecting only supporting sources.
6. Audit each decisive sentence against the actual source. Check both summary and detailed prose. Remove unsupported modifiers and reconcile contradictory caveats. Give useful bounded conclusions when certainty is unavailable; do not invent sources or quantify confidence without a basis.

For that audit, label each decisive claim as supported, explicitly contradicted, or not established by the available record. Keep absence of measurement separate from an observed zero and absence from a source collection separate from absence in the world. A report that did not collect purchase data cannot establish that nobody purchased. Preserve the distinction in charts and structured fields too: use an unknown value when the task permits it, or explain a boolean interface's limitation instead of presenting it as a measured fact.

Track corrections by fact and effective time. When a later notice changes a number, retire the old number but do not assume every old qualification remains current or has been withdrawn. For example, a revised capacity figure alone does not establish whether a preliminary review has finished. State the unresolved status and identify the source needed to resolve it. Remove contradictions between a confident opening claim and a later uncertainty note.

Example: monthly service usage rose from 200 to 260 visits, and a convenience survey of 15 current users found 6 who previously used a competing service. The record supports a 30% visit increase and the survey's six self-reports. It does not identify the source of the additional visits, establish six newly acquired users or prove substitution caused the increase. A useful next study would link consistent cohorts and periods with an appropriate comparison.

Deliver the answer with its source trail, calculations where relevant, limitations and the smallest useful next investigation. If retrieval fails, continue with available evidence while keeping the missing evidence explicit. Do not replace research with an authoritative tone.
